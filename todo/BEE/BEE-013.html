DOG-042 made [/wiki/Bro] fuse one F, and the token then resolved to NOTHING: door.js read a slash-headed ref as another repo's ROOT and tried the literal path, so it looked for a file NAMED wiki/Bro. No such file exists — the page is wiki/Bro.mkd. The reader sees a painted-but-dead reference, which is the worst of both readings — the lexer says "target", the door says "nothing".

WIP

Design decisions

A slash-headed ref is a POCKET PAGE and takes the SAME six spellings a ticket code takes, applied to the path instead of the code. The slash is DROPPED and the segments are looked up like any other ref's: a leading slash likely means that repo's root, but nothing assumes it (gritzko's ruling 2026-08-16) — we look for such a file.

  • ONE place reads the slash: refSpellings branches to pagePaths on a leading /, so every leg that resolves a partial — the reading dir, the ambient repo, the fan-out, and scanPartial's no-git walk — inherits pocket pages at once, with no leg-by-leg edit.
  • NOT ASSUMING THE ROOT is what earns the change: a wiki parked under docs/wiki/ answers /wiki/Page as readily as one at the root. The cost is that a slash-headed ref can now reach the chooser instead of missing outright, which is the honest answer: these are the files with those segments.
  • PEEL THE HEAD (ruling 2, gritzko 2026-08-16: we do not know what counts as a root in any context — projects, public_html dirs, submodules — so assume none). On a TOTAL miss, one leading segment is dropped at a time and the tail re-resolved, so /quickjab/dog/abc/TCP.c — bogus as a path, since within ///quickjab the file is dog/abc/TCP.c — opens anyway. It runs LAST, so nothing that resolves today moves, and the page-ness rides along so a peeled page keeps its spellings.
  • The peel stops at a TWO-SEGMENT tail. Peeling down to a bare basename revived every dead climb (../nowhere/q.txt found some q.txt and stopped being dead) and turned a unique miss into a chooser full of same-named files — both caught by test/http/repo.js and test/choose/pick.js on the first unbounded cut. A basename alone is too weak to vouch for a head nobody recognised.
  • anchored retired as a synonym for "starts with a slash": it now means only what ///name needs — the exact root descent under a NAMED repo — and resolvePartial carries a page flag instead. The two anchored legs page keeps are the ones that made a slash-headed ref reach the registry with no ambient repo, and skip the project-prefixed re-reading.
  • test/http/repo.js's "a leading / … does NOT match a deeper same-named file" pinned the ruling being overturned and was amended to expect the two candidates — on gritzko's say-so 2026-08-16. It is the only assertion in any suite that moved.
